Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about quantum readiness, our assessment methodology, and what to expect. Launching Q3 2026.

What is a quantum readiness assessment?

A comprehensive audit of your cryptographic infrastructure across 14 layers. Identifies every quantum-vulnerable algorithm, classifies risk by data retention and HNDL exposure, and produces a prioritized remediation roadmap aligned with NIST standards and CNSA 2.0 timelines.

How long does an assessment take?

Days to 2.5 weeks — depending on your chosen delivery tier — compared to ~13 weeks for traditional consulting audits. AI-driven scoping, 12 automated open-source scanners, and instant dashboard generation eliminate the manual steps that slow down traditional approaches.

What is PQC and why does it matter?

Post-Quantum Cryptography — algorithms designed to resist quantum computer attacks. NIST has standardized ML-KEM (FIPS 203), ML-DSA (FIPS 204), and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205). Organizations must migrate because RSA and ECDSA — which protect most encrypted data today — are broken by Shor's algorithm on a quantum computer. NIST deprecates classical algorithms by 2030, removes them by 2035.

What is Harvest Now, Decrypt Later?

Adversaries collect encrypted data today and store it until a quantum computer can decrypt it. The harvesting window opened ~2015 and closes when PQC is universal (~2035). Every month of delay = more captured data. Data with long retention (healthcare 30y, financial 7y, trade secrets) is at highest risk.

Which industries need this?

Healthcare (genomic data, PHI 30yr retention), financial services (transaction records, PCI DSS), defense contractors (CNSA 2.0 mandates from 2027), critical infrastructure (15-20yr device lifecycles), and technology companies (source code, trade secrets, code signing keys).

How does Q-Readiness compare to traditional audits?

Traditional: ~13 weeks, manual spreadsheets, static reports. Q-Readiness: 12 automated open-source scanners + AI analysis, live dashboard, days to 2.5 weeks — 80% faster. Every tool is open-source and auditable on GitHub.

What tools do you use?

12 open-source scanners: pqaudit, CryptoScan, Surveyor, pqcscan, PQC Network Scanner, liboqs, PQC-LEO, acdi, testssl.sh, database encryption discovery, Vault audit, and DNSSEC scanning. All auditable on GitHub.

What deliverables do I receive?

Cryptographic exposure map, prioritized remediation backlog (P0-P3 by HNDL urgency), executive risk memo, 90-day implementation plan, and live dashboard with drill-down evidence and PDF export.

What is CNSA 2.0 and when must I comply?

NSA's mandatory PQC requirements for national security systems. Phase 1: January 2027 — software/firmware signing must transition. Defense contractors must comply. Other organizations follow NIST: deprecation by 2030, removal by 2035.

Do you support air-gapped environments?

Yes. Q-Appliance (Tier 4) has no network interface — WiFi slot empty, Ethernet TX trace cut, verifiable by you. Tier 0 (Self-Service) and Tier 3 (Expert On-Site) also support air-gapped deployment.

How is my data protected?

Data stays in your environment. All scanners run locally — nothing leaves without written approval. Open-source toolchain means every line is auditable. Q-Appliance has no network capability. Remote assessments use read-only access with end-to-end encryption.
